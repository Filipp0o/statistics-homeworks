03 / Limit theorems

Concentration and fluctuation.

LLN concerns the average approaching a constant; CLT describes the distribution of fluctuations on the square-root scale.

Law of Large Numbers

Averages become reliable.

For every fixed ε > 0, Sn/n converges in probability to μ. Estimate the deviation probability by counting attackers whose averages miss μ by more than ε.

P(|Sn/n − μ| > ε) → 0Chebyshev bound: min(1, σ² / (nε²))

Central Limit Theorem

A stable distribution emerges.

Rademacher increments have μ = 0 and σ² = 1. Their normalized total converges in distribution to the standard normal:

Sn / √n ⇒ N(0,1)Rademacher model

Compare the normalized histogram with its orange normal reference as n increases. Its shape approaches the reference while its variance stays near one. A normalized trajectory can keep fluctuating; it does not settle at a fixed normal value.

For Bernoulli, E[Sn/√n] = p√n: for p > 0 the mean drifts right, so this uncentered quantity does not tend to N(0,1). At p = 0 or 1 the process is deterministic.

Finite samples need not improve at every checkpoint. Zero observed deviations do not prove a zero true probability; a simulated fraction can exceed the Chebyshev bound through sampling noise. More attackers improve the empirical estimates.

Increasing n changes each attacker’s distribution. Increasing m supplies more independent trajectories without changing that individual law. In Bernoulli, the expected aggregate grows as mnp; in Rademacher it stays zero because positive and negative scores cancel on average. For either model, aggregate standard deviation is √(mnσ²), while pooled-average standard deviation is √(σ²/(mn)).

05 / Model and method

From increments to scores.

For attacker a at system i, let Xa,i be the increment. Its total is Sa,n = Σi=1n Xa,i.

A count of successes

Bernoulli increments

X = 1 with probability p and X = 0 with probability 1 − p. The score counts successful attacker–system attempts, rather than distinct systems breached.

μ = p   ·   σ² = p(1 − p)Sn ∼ Binomial(n,p)
Progress and setbacks

Rademacher increments

X = +1 or −1, each with probability 1/2. The score measures signed net progress and can be negative.

μ = 0   ·   σ² = 1Sn has the distribution of 2B − n, B ∼ Binomial(n,1/2).

Write μ = E[X] and σ² = Var(X). Independence makes variances add; dividing a random variable by a constant divides its variance by the square of that constant.

Simulation procedure

Generate increments up to the largest n for every attacker and store the totals at the chosen checkpoints. Increasing n extends the same paths. Both models use separate seeded pseudorandom streams. The reference experiment uses 2,000 attackers and n = 10, 50, 100, 500, 1,000.

Assumptions

Increments are IID across systems, and attackers are independent. The models exclude shared defenses, adaptation and interactions between attackers. They describe simulated penetration scores, not measurements of real intrusions.
